Designing a driver experience that minimizes friction and keeps attention on the road via the platform Smart Dash —

UX/UI Designer & Area LeadScaniaJan 2023 – NowContinuous DevelopmentNDA restricted

Summary

Smart Dash was developed to create a safer, smarter and more connected driving experience by bringing together the vehicle’s digital functions in a common platform. I have contributed to the development through all phases of the design process - from exploring user needs to designing, testing, and refining UX/UI solutions. I have also contributed to the design system and broader interaction patterns, helping create a more consistent and scalable experience.

My role

  • Delivering end-to-end UX design for digital interactions with vehicle functions
  • Developing reusable patterns and frameworks used across the platform
  • Collaborating cross-functionally in an agile, global context
  • Guiding design explorations and supporting other designers within my domain

Outcome

  • Delivered, validated, and refined numerous HMI experiences now used by thousands of professional drivers every day
  • Established interaction patterns, guidelines and frameworks adopted across multiple features
  • Helped establish ways of working for a newly formed team, created onboarding material and supported designers in adopting established HMI workflows
  • Coordinated upcoming initiatives and influenced interaction strategy within a key HMI domain, balancing immediate feature needs with long-term strategy

Challenge

Designing for professional drivers operating in contexts where attention is scarce and safety is key

Every interaction must be carefully designed to minimize cognitive load and avoid distraction. This makes prioritization of information and clarity of interaction central to the design.

The domain is also complex. Designs must account for varying hardware capabilities and constraints, as well as regulatory requirements and safety standards.

Designing in-vehicle interfaces for trucks and buses also means that we design across a wide range of applications with significantly different user needs and use cases. Solutions must therefore be adaptable while maintaining consistency and usability.

Design process

I start by making sure that I understand the problem

The approach to reach this understanding varies depending on how well understood the problem is. Sometimes it requires in-depth user research, while in other cases it is more about alignment and benchmarking to establish a shared understanding.

I explore concepts when the problem is understood

Depending on the context, the exploration phase may involve sketching, wireframing, or prototyping, with ideas iteratively refined through stakeholder feedback and design reviews.

I use testing to identify the strongest solution when the direction is not clear

Testing plays an important role in identifying the strongest solution. The level of testing may however vary depending on factors such as timelines and novelty of the concept. Regardless, I make sure that solutions are evaluated - whether within the design team, with stakeholders, or with users.

I progress to detailed design when the direction is set

This phase often involves collaboration with other related design roles to further detail the design.

I align cross-functionally and with stakeholders throughout the whole process

This is necessary to make sure that the design is understood and so that we can address uncertainties when they arise, all to ensure a high-quality solution.

What I learned

Working in this domain has trained me to balance several perspectives and constraints

I have learned to balance the user’s immediate needs, the safety implications of an interaction, technical and hardware constraints, regulatory requirements, and how a solution needs to work across a much larger platform.

I have shifted from feature-level thinking to system-level thinking

As my role has evolved and I have taken ownership of a dedicated area, I have learned to consider how features relate to and depend on one another, and how individual decisions shape the system as a whole.

I have learned to question requests rather than automatically turn them into solutions

In an in-vehicle environment, every feature competes for the driver's limited attention, making it essential to carefully consider what deserves to be included. Rather than simply making a request work, I have learned to investigate its purpose, challenge its assumptions, and weigh its value against the complexity it introduces. This has made knowing when to say no, and being able to explain why, an increasingly important part of my design process.

Smart Components

Improving the usability of Smart Components in RobotStudio through redesign of a graphical programming interface —

Master’s Thesis in Interaction DesignABBJan 2022 – June 2022

Summary

I did my master’s thesis at ABB Robotics together with a classmate, where we redesigned the Smart Components experience in RobotStudio to make a complex technical workflow easier and more efficient to use. Through research, iterative concept development, prototyping, and usability testing with professional robot programmers, the result was a redesigned interface that was perceived as a clear improvement over the existing one.

My role

  • User research with professional RobotStudio users
  • UX/UI design from early concepts and low-fidelity prototypes to a high-fidelity interactive prototype
  • Iterative usability evaluation throughout the design process, including a final evaluation with four expert users

Outcome

  • A redesigned interface to build Smart Components, that scored 78/100 on the System Usability Scale (SUS) and was perceived as a clear improvement over the existing interface

Background

Smart Components is a feature that enables professional robot programmers to simulate behaviors in robotic stations

The feature exists in RobotStudio, the world’s most widely used robot programming tool. To simulate behaviors with Smart Components, the user has to connect building blocks representing specific functions in a graphical programming interface.

Challenge

The feature was perceived as difficult and cumbersome to use, but why was not clear

Before the start of this project, Smart Components were already widely adopted and played an important role in robot programmers’ workflows. However, despite their reliance on the functionality, there had been recurring feedback that working with Smart Components was difficult. This established a clear starting point for the project: the challenge was to understand what aspects of the experience made it difficult, and where there was potential to improve it.

Pain points

Users had to manage many interconnected subcomponents by navigating between several views and without being able to get an overview

To reach this understanding, we reviewed both the existing interface and the context in which Smart Components were used. I did literature research, studied the existing interface in RobotStudio and conducted interviews with professional users to reveal where users experienced friction. We translated these insights into a set of design requirements used as a basis for the upcoming exploration phase.

Focus of the exploration

Helping users manage complexity and get an overview without removing or hiding required functionality

The exploration took off focusing on the question above. This led to concepts around grouping, visual differentiation, responsive components and reducing the amount of navigation required. The concepts were repeatedly evaluated and refined before moving into high-fidelity design.

Key decision

Bringing the functions into one coherent design editor instead of presenting them in different views

The redesigned interface brought together the tools needed to create, connect and configure Smart Components while retaining the functionality users already relied on. Additionally, we introduced new features in the interface to even further improve the overview and flexibility.

New interface features

Responsiveness

To improve the overview when managing many interconnected components, we introduced responsive components that reveal different levels of detail depending on zoom level.

Color-coding

To make properties and signals associated with the components easier to distinguish in an overview, we introduced color-coding.

Grouping

To make it easier to organize and reuse components, we introduced a feature to group related sub components. This was also a way to reduce clutter.

Expand and collapse

Allowing the users to expand and collapse panels as needed was intended to give users more control over the workspace.

Search

To help with findability in complex components, we introduced a search function.

Result

Users rated the new interface positively, highlighting improved visibility and workflow efficiency

The final high-fidelity prototype was evaluated with four professional RobotStudio users through the System Usability Scale (SUS) and semi-structured interviews. The interface received a mean SUS score of 78/100, and participants generally considered it an improvement over the existing interface. They particularly valued having all tools accessible in one view, while grouping and responsive components were seen as effective ways to manage complexity and support more efficient workflows.

What I learned

Usability does not necessarily mean simplicity

Professional users often need access to sophisticated functionality. The challenge is therefore to understand the complexity they are already working with and make it easier to navigate, rather than simply hiding or reducing it. This led to a change in perspective, from: “How can I make this simpler?” to “How can I help the user manage the complexity?”

There are problems that cannot be addressed by the UI alone

The evaluation of the final design concept revealed an important limitation in the solution. While the graphical interface could make Smart Components easier to organise and navigate, some of the underlying complexity came from the low-level programming model itself: users still needed to combine many highly specific building blocks to create relatively complex behaviour. No amount of interface refinement could completely solve that underlying issue.

Users are an essential source of knowledge when systems rely on domain expertise

The project also gave me my first deeper experience of designing for highly specialised professional users. Rather than assuming that I could become an expert in their domain myself, I learned to use users and domain experts as an essential source of knowledge throughout the process. My role was to understand their mental models and workflows well enough to identify where design could genuinely support them. That experience has stayed with me in my later work with complex professional tools, where domain knowledge, user expertise and design expertise need to meet rather than replace one another.
